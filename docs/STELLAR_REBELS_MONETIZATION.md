# Stellar Rebels monetization decision

Build a genuinely free playable core first. The initial release has no ads. Decide on checkout only from the finished game. The current content evidence is a one-stage slice. There is no demonstrated paid-content value or approved launch price. If the remaining game provides a substantially different experience and players can recognize its value, prefer the owner's generous free-to-start model with one permanent purchase. If the finished game remains a compact free arc with a slight extension, release the complete core free.

| Model | Free player receives | What could justify payment | Cost and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A · Generous free-to-start with permanent unlock | A self-contained route with distinct bosses, playable ship variety, spendable earned Salvage, mastery and Endless | A substantial second route with distinct tactical problems, new scenes and bosses, a final encounter and meaningful replay. A lower price is a test candidate if the addition is compact. | One non-consumable per store plus sandbox restore and entitlement support. A gate based only on stage count risks asking for money before players see enough variety. |
| B · Complete free core | The entire finished campaign, all ships and mastery, shared upgrades and secondary Endless | A future optional livery offering or a separately built substantial expansion. Any later voluntary advertising first needs review. | Lowest disruption and engineering cost today. Initial proceeds may be zero. Optional purchases can be unattractive and future ad economics are unknown. |
| C · Hybrid over time | The generous free route from A, with identical play conditions if the player ignores later offers | Permanent full-content unlock at launch, then separately approved optional cosmetic-reward placements for interested free players | Contains all purchase support costs plus later policy, consent, performance and QA work. The value and experience cost of each component need independent evidence. |

**Recommended sequence.** Validate stock combat and mastery first. Give free players a route with a real ending of its own, repeatable stages, permanent upgrades and ship sidegrades that remain earnable without payment. Test one permanent full-content purchase only when the extra content has distinct, demonstrable value. Test a price around US $2.99 or $4.99 against the finished package. Both numbers are hypotheses. Show an offer once at a natural point such as completion of the free arc. Replay and Endless remain immediately available. Preserve upgrades, difficulty and records across entitlements. Show only actual finished content and the price returned by the applicable store.

**Purchase evidence.** Observe whether first-time players voluntarily retry and pursue a different tactical outcome without financial rewards, then whether they can describe exactly what the expansion adds. Show them genuine clips of the free and proposed paid encounters and ask which they would pay for at an explicit price. Record counts and reasons as directional findings. Player behavior at a real price is still unmeasured until a compliant sale or price test happens. The reference video and competitors supply ideas without isolating what caused purchase or retention.

**Store implementation, if selected.** Use a real non-consumable StoreKit and Google Play Billing purchase for the native game under typical store rules. Handle pending, purchased, restore, canceled and revoked transactions. Grant one entitlement only after verified purchase, acknowledge Google Play delivery, preserve access after reinstall or device change, and test first launch, failure, restoration and refund with real sandbox purchases. Keep offline access to previously validated content with controlled revalidation. Re-check policies for the intended storefront at release. [Apple IAP setup](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/configure-in-app-purchase-settings/overview-for-configuring-in-app-purchases) · [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#in-app-purchase) · [Google Play one-time purchase lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/one-time)

**After launch only.** Consider AdMob once the published native game can pass the platform and AdMob reviews and the owner approves a concrete placement. Keep combat, pause, Retry, mastery, currency, rewards and purchases independent of availability or ad completion. If evidence supports testing one placement, make it an explicit optional action in Hangar. A preview of an alternate paint treatment is one possible reward. Keep paid entitlements and the ad-free experience promised to early purchasers. An unavailable ad leaves the game and progression fully usable. Ads and ad-dependent code are absent from the initial release. No approval, fill rate or revenue is assumed. [AdMob app readiness](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/10564477?hl=en)

**Budget.** Total incremental spending may not exceed $15. So far it is $0. Do not buy traffic, ad SDKs, additional content tools, native build credits or new memberships to make this release possible. Fresh program enrollment alone is $99/year for Apple or $25 once for Google Play. Use existing eligible accounts if available. Expo EAS is reserved for a release-quality candidate.
