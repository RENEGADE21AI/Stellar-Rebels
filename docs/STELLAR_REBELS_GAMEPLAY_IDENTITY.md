# Stellar Rebels gameplay identity and playtest plan

On November 19, product steering put the first minute and monetization ahead of content count. This document records the comparison before further encounter or fleet production.

## What the references teach

Infinite Dreams' Sky Force Reloaded pairs authored missions with objectives, several difficulty tiers and differentiated aircraft. It makes the next try legible. Its 15 stages and extensive sub-systems represent a different production scale. For Stellar Rebels, keep one concrete mastery choice inside each authored encounter, visible in the recap, and use a finite shared upgrade set. There is no evidence here that copying the progression magnitude would improve this game. [Developer’s Sky Force Reloaded listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/667600/Sky_Force_Reloaded/)

Firi's Phoenix 2 differentiates ships by weapon and ability combinations, and gives returning players a shared daily challenge. Different attack footprints make positioning consequential. The scale of its daily, social and collection systems is unsuitable here. For Stellar Rebels, make walking a narrow stream onto a hardpoint, or covering a broken wing with a spread, change what the pilot aims at. Use hand-authored roles and a local performance record. [Firi Games’ Phoenix 2 overview](https://firigames.com/phoenix2)

The first production encounter must teach its rules through formation movement, a short one-time cue and a clear outcome. Progress through the remaining stages only when the slice demonstrates a concrete reason to return. Keep product slots 2 and 3 reserved for the actual three-stage build and later release candidate. ## Stock comparison and decision

The first authored input pass found that the moving command ship died before its vane could be reliably targeted, and a stationary lane caused all three attempts to fail near 33 seconds. The revision steadied the command ship for its first charge, left the main body targetable and made lateral aim legible. A second pass used 390 × 844 Chromium, portrait touch deltas and controlled browser animation time. It held the same stock loadout and stage schedule. Only the comparison harness exposes the ability candidate.

| Trial | At first contact near 17.8 s | At the one-minute mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary | Ordinary body kill and nine active hostile shots | 27 kills, 5,537 score and two hull |
| Command vane | Vane destroyed in the charge. Three escort craft jammed, and three hostile shots were still in play. | 27 kills, 5,630 score and one hull |
| Ghost Shift | One extra press and a successful travel-triggered counter volley. No interception of the coordinated pattern. | 27 kills, 5,105 score and one hull |

Keep the interrupt as one optional encounter behavior. It yielded a recognizable target choice and opened a moment for attacking three exposed enemies with the same drag/auto-fire controls. Direct body kills remain useful. There is no standalone mode, tree, button or required streak for it. Reserve the Ghost Shift idea for a future problem that warrants a second combat input.

A deterministic pilot cleared Vesper Orbit in 180.4 seconds with stock equipment and 170.9 seconds with every upgrade. It steered and aimed with the normal speed limits. Ending hull was five and six respectively. This establishes a legal clear and shows that the boss still attacks with full upgrades. It is easier to respond with deterministic prediction than on a real phone. Browser and author play identify promising decisions and feedback. Representative player willingness to retry, actual comfort on physical phones and retention remain unmeasured.
